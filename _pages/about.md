I am a historian of U.S.–Russian relations, American foreign policy, and the politics of memory. My research covers the international dimensions of the American Civil War, the (mis)use of history in contemporary Russia, and the long history of mutual perceptions between Russia and the United States.

My book *Americans and All the Rest: Origin and Meaning of the U.S. Foreign Policy* (Alpina, 2024) received the **Prosvetitel** award for the best Russian-language non-fiction book of 2024; an English translation is forthcoming from Palgrave Macmillan (June 2026). With David S. Foglesong and Victoria Zhuravleva, I co-authored *Distant Friends and Intimate Enemies: A History of American–Russian Relations* (Cambridge University Press, 2025). *Battle for the Past: How Politics Rewrites History* appeared from Palgrave Macmillan in 2025.

I am currently Visiting Professor of History at The Ohio State University (2025–2026), where I teach courses on U.S.–Russian relations and the global dimensions of the American Civil War. Previously, I was Professor at the European University at St. Petersburg (2015–2024), Head of the Department of International Relations and Area Studies at Volgograd State University (2006–2015), and have held visiting appointments at Wellesley College, Bowdoin College, Dartmouth, George Washington University, and Middlebury College.

In March 2024 I was dismissed from the European University at St. Petersburg after publicly opposing Russia’s war against Ukraine. In January 2026 I was designated a "foreign agent" by the Russian Ministry of Justice.
